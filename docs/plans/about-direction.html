What is The Kinetic Alphabet?

A revised direction for About: first-read explanation and project authorship in one page, with a clear handoff to the software tour.

Decision status

Draft strategy, not approved biography. The product framing below is proposed. Creator motivation, portrait, performance media, testimonials, adoption, and outcome claims are deliberately absent until supplied and permissioned.

Recommendation

Make About the answer to the homepage question, then make its boundary useful.

The homepage already sends a stranger to “What is TKA?” after showing a live sequence and notation strip. About should give that visitor the missing meaning: TKA is a pictographic notation for flow-arts choreography, and one picture records a movement step so a sequence can be revisited, compared, changed, or shared. It should then state the project’s purpose, realistic prop scope, authorship, and next door.

A compact picture-and-sequence explanation honors the incoming promise. Give the creator room to explain the project, and leave step-by-step instruction to the Guide. Alpha, Beta, and Gamma can enter when someone chooses to learn the notation.

Primary next step: Explore Flow Arts Composer.

For a curious cold visitor, the software tour sets expectations before the direct application. Keep Guide secondary and Open Flow Arts Composer available but visually quieter. This is a product judgment, not a conversion claim.

Decision record

Keep: the direct definition, creator credit, 2022 date, and grant source. Change: lead with explanation and purpose, then scope. Defer: portrait, origin story in Austen’s voice, social proof, and any background effect fix.

Story outline

Five beats, each with one job.

  1. Hero, “What is The Kinetic Alphabet?” Direct definition. Explain the distinction between the notation and Flow Arts Composer.
  2. Purpose with one real artifact. Place an existing sequence image or player beside one use case: preserve a phrase, change a step, and compare what follows. Video remains useful; no replacement or superiority promise. Any animation starts only when the visitor asks.
  3. Scope. Double staves are canonical. The notation can apply to dual static props such as fans, clubs, and buugeng; a Composer visual is not a claim of universal physical validity.
  4. Creator and project. Credit Austen Cloud, state 2022, and link the 2024–25 Seed Fund grant beside that fact. Do not invent motives.
  5. One next step. Lead to Composer’s tour; retain Guide, History, FAQ, and a quiet direct-app link for the visitor who already knows what they want.
Proposed copy, grounded in current About and FAQ

Short enough to leave room for the artifact.

Hero

“The Kinetic Alphabet is a way to write flow arts choreography in pictures. Each picture records a movement step. Put them together to record a sequence. Flow Arts Composer is the browser app for composing, playing, and sharing sequences in this notation.”

Purpose

“A video can show a performance. TKA gives a sequence a form you can return to, change, compare, and pass along.”

Scope

“TKA was designed around double staves. It also applies to paired static props such as fans, clubs, and buugeng. Tosses, contact rolling, grip changes, and momentum-based props are outside that core scope. A prop’s appearance in Composer does not establish that every sequence can be performed with it.”

Factual boundaries come from the current About page and the canonical FAQ, checked against their repository sources. These are draft lines for review. The purpose line proposes product framing; Austen’s personal origin story still needs his own words.

Adversarial corrections

Corrections to the audit, and risks in the revision.

Earlier finding or revision risk Better treatment
Correction: the page allegedly omits its purpose. Current copy already explains keeping choreography readable instead of scattered across video, notes, and memory. Make that purpose concrete and include composing new work.
Risk: the proposed annotated example grows into another lesson. The “See how a pictograph reads” link does overpromise its text-only destination. Relabel it or add one compact example. The Guide owns instruction; About still answers the homepage’s “What is TKA?” link.
Risk: asking the grant to prove too much. Keep the independent award source. Add evidence of usefulness through a real sequence, without implying the award verifies effectiveness.
Risk: treating a thin creator section as a demand for a portrait or social proof. The useful missing material is the project’s origin in Austen’s voice. A portrait and testimonials are optional; invented motives and usage figures have no place here.
Qualification: inferred confusion and visual distraction. Greek-letter confusion is a plausible hypothesis, not an observed user finding. Starfield distraction is a visual judgment; no contrast failure was measured. Cached service-worker performance cannot support speed claims.
Reduced-motion screenshots, a zoom proxy, or successful HTTP prove behavior. A hash does not isolate motion. Test the canvas and initial-load preference. A half-width viewport is not actual 200% browser zoom. HTTP success does not verify the destination experience.
Why the tour, not direct Create, is primary

The homepage may rightly offer direct creation. On About, a visitor has asked for meaning after seeing an animated example. A tour is the more legible first handoff because it explains the app before asking for action. This remains a recommendation to test, not evidence of improved conversion.

Acceptance and open inputs

A stranger should be able to say what the notation is versus what the app is, name a use case and realistic prop scope, identify the author, and choose a next step. Optional inputs that enrich, but never block, the factually grounded version: Austen’s own two- or three-sentence origin and permissioned portrait or performance media.
