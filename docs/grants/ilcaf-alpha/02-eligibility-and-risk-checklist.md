## Framing risks

### Software-development disqualification

The current repository is extensive, but the program explicitly excludes
software development as an artistic practice. Do not lead with "software
developer," a coding roadmap, application features, or a grant-funded build.
Composer may show how Austen presents and circulates choreography, just as a
camera or editing suite could support a media artist.

### Unsupported duration claims

The repository bio says Taco Tuesday Flow Jams began in 2017. Email evidence
independently corroborates the activity beginning in 2018 and continued
leadership by 2021. Use "since 2017" only after Austen confirms it; keep the
2018 email as the earliest dated record in this package.

### Work-sample ownership

The recent video includes other performers. Austen must identify who filmed,
edited, choreographed, taught, and performed, and confirm permission to submit
the video. The description must state his role without claiming collaborators'
work as his own.

### AI disclosure

The work-sample files predate the current generative-image experiments found
elsewhere on the computer, but Austen must still confirm whether any image,
text, editing, or notation in either selected sample used AI assistance.

### Public link reliability

The recent sample's source file is 676 MB, so it cannot be uploaded directly.
Before submission, either publish an accessible link or produce a visually
faithful derivative under 25 MB. Test the link in a signed-out browser.
