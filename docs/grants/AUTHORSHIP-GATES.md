# Grant Authorship Gates

These gates govern every prose response written for Austen Cloud's grant
applications. They apply to drafts, revisions, portal text, biographies, work
sample descriptions, budgets with narrative fields, and supporting statements.

## The gate sequence

No step may be skipped.

1. **Lock the prompt.** Record the exact question, limit, reviewer criteria,
   and any facts the funder requires.
2. **Interview Austen.** Ask focused questions for this response. Austen may
   answer casually or by voice transcription. Do not draft before he answers.
3. **Build the source map.** Put Austen's new answers beside relevant passages
   from his earlier writing or speech. Mark every factual claim with its source.
4. **Propose the angle.** Explain how the old and new material could answer the
   prompt. This is an outline, not application prose.
5. **Get angle approval.** Austen must approve the direction before drafting.
6. **Draft from approved material.** Preserve Austen's phrasing where it is
   clear. Add connective language only where the prompt requires it.
7. **Run the authorship review.** Check the draft with the AI-bust rules, read
   it aloud for sentence rhythm, compare it with the source map, and flag any
   sentence that did not come from Austen's facts or approved direction.
8. **Show the evidence.** Present the draft, word and character counts, source
   map, AI-bust results, and any remaining uncertainty.
9. **Get prose approval.** Austen must explicitly approve the final wording.
10. **Save and enter.** Save the approved draft and enter it in the portal only
    after the approval. Austen controls every final submission action.

## Required source priority

Use sources in this order:

1. Austen's current interview answer
2. Austen's recorded speech and tutorial transcripts
3. Austen's earlier first-person writing
4. Previously approved grant language
5. Verified repository facts

Do not treat old AI-assisted copy as Austen's voice merely because it is in the
repository. It may provide verified facts, but its phrasing does not enter the
draft unless Austen approves it.

## Rejection conditions

A response returns to the interview or angle gate when it contains:

- a claim without a source;
- language Austen would not say aloud;
- a generic sentence that could fit another applicant;
- an AI-bust violation;
- a factual or scope overclaim;
- wording that answers a nearby question instead of the actual prompt;
- a sentence added only to sound impressive;
- a material change made after Austen's approval.

## Portal boundary

Agents may inspect forms and enter approved, non-sensitive text after receiving
action-time permission. Austen enters authentication codes and sensitive
identity information. Austen reviews certifications and presses the final
submission control.
