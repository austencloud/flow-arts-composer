## Wall Plaque Text

### Main Plaque

```
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

                    FRAGMENT OF THE LASCAUX
                      NOTATION TABLETS

                 Dordogne, France. c. 35,000 BCE
                    Limestone, ochre pigment

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Discovered in 1979 by speleologist Dr. Henri Marchand in a
  previously sealed chamber adjacent to the famous Lascaux cave
  paintings, these tablets represent the earliest known
  systematic notation of coordinated movement.

  The sequence depicted—now catalogued as "OOGA-1" in the
  Proto-Kinetic Index—shows a four-beat pattern using what
  appear to be burning branches. Analysis of wear patterns
  suggests these tablets were handled frequently, likely for
  instructional purposes.

  Dr. Marchand's field notes, published posthumously, describe
  the chamber as having "an almost ceremonial quality" with
  "evidence of repeated gathering." He hypothesized a
  "rehearsal space" where early humans practiced firelit
  performances before presenting them to the larger community.

  The simpler tablet—catalogued as "UG-1"—depicts a basic
  alpha-to-alpha transition, believed to be a foundational
  teaching sequence.

  Carbon dating places these artifacts among the oldest known
  examples of systematic notation of any kind, predating
  written language by approximately 30,000 years.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  On loan from the Marchand Estate
  Photography prohibited

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
```

### Secondary Plaque (Smaller, to the Side)

```
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  ACADEMIC NOTE

  The authenticity of the Lascaux Tablets remains
  contested by some scholars. Dr. Elena Voss of
  the Cairo Institute for Kinetic Studies argues
  that "the systematic nature of the notation
  suggests a level of cognitive sophistication
  inconsistent with current models of Paleolithic
  development."

  Others, including the late Prof. Marcus Chen-Okonkwo
  of Harwick University, have noted that "the
  absence of these tablets from mainstream
  archaeological literature raises troubling
  questions about institutional suppression of
  inconvenient evidence."

  The Marchand Estate has declined requests for
  independent verification since 1987.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
```

- Larger of the two (~30cm x 20cm)

- Primitive but clearly systematic
