### The jam slice: Dusk at Middle Earth

`Launch-Jam.cmd` (or `.\Tools\launch.ps1 -Jam`) starts only Thursday's fire-circle jam. Placements come from `Content/FlowFest/Assets/Jam/Data/jam-layout.json`, which the browser simulation exports from `src/lib/features/flow-fest-sim/domain/flow-fest-jam-layout.ts`. A dusk loading screen stays up until every performer, watcher and safety is dressed and posed with their prop, shaders and assets are done, and streaming is idle; the log lists ready and expected counts per group and the time to the reveal. The opening shot follows the layout's camera keys under the title card (any key skips it), then hands over to the third-person camera at the funnel, and the night builds over about four minutes (`-NightSeconds` changes that). Stand still near the rope and the camera eases back. **E** sits at the edge, and **E** or movement stands you up. Walking in through the gap lights your prop. **Q** flows: after 6 seconds the people near you turn to watch, and after 18 the nearest performer comes over and plays in step with you. The slice is silent, because the festival's ambience loops are synthesised; recorded night ambience is the planned replacement. It never writes over the journey's save. `.\Tools\launch.ps1 -Jam -Watch` keeps the camera, records 40 seconds at 1920×1080 with a 30-second night, and encodes `Saved/Polish/Jam/jam-watch.mp4` with ffmpeg, or keeps eight stills if ffmpeg is missing. Every body except the player's and the spinners' updates at a rate set by its size on screen. Spinners playing a routine evaluate every frame, because their prop moves on only when their body evaluates: on the size-on-screen rate they posed on about one frame in six, 8 to 10 times a second, and with every frame they pose 43 to 60 times a second with no change in frame time (`-FlowFestJamThrottleSpinners` brings the old rate back to measure against; the stat line reports spinner poses per second). The slice also budgets the shared arm clearance, which is the expensive part of each evaluation. Watchers with empty hands skip it, the duet partner and the one spinner nearest the camera (within 9 m) keep the exact solve, and everyone else uses the cheaper capsule check. A body made exact stays exact for at least 6 seconds and lets go only past 12 m, so the choice rarely changes. Nobody in the crowd casts a shadow, and fire more than 18 m from the camera pauses. The slice runs at High scalability with the moving sun's shadow pages drawn at half resolution, and dynamic resolution may drop to 70% (upscaled by TSR) when the GPU runs over. While the light falls in the opening, the camera lets exposure adapt one stop further so the picture stays readable, then hands back to the normal night look once the circle is lit. For measurement, `-FlowFestJamExactBodies=<n>` sets how many spinners keep the exact solve, `-FlowFestJamLevers=<mask>` picks the levers (1 clearance, 2 shadows, 4 fire, 8 scalability; all on by default), and `-FlowFestJamFireDistance=<cm>` moves the fire cut-off. With the development PC otherwise quiet, the editor `-game` build at 1600×900 holds game, draw and GPU near 12 to 16 ms in the opening and at full night; other work on the machine pushes the game thread over 16.7 ms.

## Play

Press **C** to choose your character and equipment. All 16 deployed characters and all 71 prop variants are available; selections save with the journey. Regular and large buugeng each offer four left/right pairings: matching A, mirrored AB, mirrored BA, and matching B. Choose a departure with **1**, **2**, or **3**, then press **E**. Drive to the gate, check in, and choose lower tent, upper tent, or car camp. Arrive and unload; tent campers stage the car at parking and walk home. Make camp, visit the workshop field, follow the lights to the fire circle, flow in the jam, then return to camp and sleep. Friday morning ends the scripted first-night journey.

## Native scope and limits

The native world builds terrain, roads, trees, camps, parked cars, landmarks, visitors, fire, sky, and fog from exported site data and imported assets. The ecology export adds 21,731 grass placements and 20 damp-sedge and hazel understory placements, with Dynamic Wind on the Nanite Foliage trees and ground surface layers. Each grass placement grows a voxelized Nanite meadow patch (`Assets/Meadow`), ringed by stubble tufts, with dry seed stalks in tall or parched spots. The source keeps only two of three cells on a 4 m grid outside the camp lawns, so about 23,600 fill patches on a jittered 1.6 m grid cover the open ground between, each borrowing its nearest placement's shade and tint and thinning out 3.5 to 6 m past the last one; per-instance custom data sets each clump's dryness and brightness from the source tint, shade, neighbour density and trampling. Grass is cleared under parked cars and flattened beside them and the roads, and the field ground dries toward straw in broad patches to match the hills. Under the measured trees the forest floor fills in where the crowns shade the ground: about 11,000 leaf-litter drifts, ferns in 10 to 20 m drifts, hazel at the brighter edges, and dead branches, fallen logs and stumps around the trunks, all placed on the rendered terrain surface rather than the finer height grid, which the 2 m terrain triangles bury small things beneath. The ground darkens toward damp soil under the crowns, carried in the terrain's vertex colour, and its detail textures are flattened and sampled twice at a turned scale, so the far woods show no repeating stripes. The source keeps only a quarter of the trees the LiDAR canopy-height scan shows, so the woods fill themselves in from that scan: about 9,900 more trees on a jittered 5 m grid wherever the canopy stands 6 m or taller and no crown already shades the spot, each as tall as the scan says, kept 3.6 m from any trunk, off roads, camps and landmarks, and drawn with the backdrop trees of its nearest family. Tall canopy under a crown goes from 25% to 90%. Open land the ground mask calls litter is farmland: `Tools/classify-fields.mjs` reads the orthophoto's colour there into a field weight and a dryness, and the ground paints green crop rows about 76 cm apart on the moist fields and straw-coloured hay and stubble on the dry ones, keeping each real field's tracks from the photo's light and dark. Within 260 m of the festival about 2,300 short hay tufts grow on the dry fields. Each road and path is one strip along a curve through its surveyed points, sampled every metre and laid on the rendered terrain with the terrain's own normals, so bends are smooth and the surface neither floats nor sinks. The county road is worn tar that crumbles into a gravel shoulder; the camp drives are packed soil with two wheel ruts and a grassy crown, and the foot paths wear one paler line. Every shoulder thins raggedly into the grass through a dithered opacity mask, each end fades over half a width so a drive joins its road without a square cap, and the road materials darken under the crowns like the ground. The sky has volumetric fair-weather cumulus that shade the field, Mie haze and stretched aerial perspective so the far tree lines sit back, height fog that glows toward a low sun, and a grade that warms highlights and cools shade as the sun drops through the afternoon. The sun follows a late-summer path for the site's 43 degrees north, rising east-northeast, crossing due south at noon and setting west-northwest, so shadows swing through the day and the camp falls into the western tree line's shade by early evening. The meadow material needs both the Nanite and Voxels usage flags, or voxelized clumps draw the default material and render black. Native attendee schedules use 38 named crowd members with route following, social stops, and rotating fire turns. Six original procedural mono loops provide woodland, camp, crowd, and fire ambience with distance attenuation, configured occlusion, and gains that change with time of day and jam state. The HUD and C++ state machine cover loadout, arrival, gate check-in, three camp choices, camp setup, workshop field, fire jam, and Friday morning. The character system retains the 16 deployed meshes and 11 locomotion clips per rig. Props retain all 71 authored variants, hand colors, and physical dimensions.

The attendee simulation gives the 38 attendees bounded needs, preferences, homes, equipment, and inspectable goals. They travel to food, camp, practice, watch, socialize in pairs, or take reserved turns at the fire circle. Needs and completed actions affect the next goal; ongoing routes, actions, and fire reservations are saved. Practice and performance use authored prop routines. Food and rest now use shared eating and ground-sitting poses. Resting attendees complete their standing transition before their next journey; intentions, activity clocks, and that delay survive save/load. Social stops retain their destination, facing, and timing without a dedicated talking animation. Stowed equipment has no visible holster.

The food stop stands on the walk from the lower loop into the workshop field, beside the first-aid canopy, with attendees arriving at it; the player sees an E food/water prompt. The white food pavilion and its two booths stand at the field's west edge. Carrying holds equipped props near the torso over locomotion. The native runtime measures imported skin and fitted prop covers, applies bounded contact correction, and reports unresolved collisions. A 60-frame capture used the actual Walk clip at 185 cm/s; same-frame transforms kept each prop fixed to its hand within 0.000003 cm. Separate 60-frame sit and rise captures cover the transition. Eating's sampled x-bot, ch34, and ch44 poses had no exact upper-arm/torso crossings, although conservative body envelopes still report some residuals. Dense spinning cycles and the final package have not passed collision acceptance. See [LIVE-MOTION.md](LIVE-MOTION.md) for the current correction policy and limits.

The scripted experience covers the first night. Car and EUC movement remains simple; the car has no Chaos vehicle physics. The EUC uses the production mounted contact pose. Each of the 41 prop families retains two packaged routines. New valid Composer `SequenceData` or verified performance transfers can also be captured externally and loaded through F6 for the selected character and exact prop variant; Unreal plays the captured motion without evaluating TKA notation itself. Camp dressing, visitor behavior, and fire use native implementations. Photo flight and travel near objective are review aids.

## Asset provenance

### Shared native foundation

Flow Fest and Museum use the `FlowArtsRuntime` plugin under `unreal/Shared/FlowArtsRuntime`. It owns native avatar animation, equipment, grip IK, the synchronized playback clock, and routine transitions. `unreal/Shared/Tools` owns character catalog evaluation and capture validation. Festival geography, journeys, attendee schedules, and UI remain game-specific. Flow Fest's project enables the plugin through `AdditionalPluginDirectories: ["../Shared"]` and redirects its former game-module component names to the shared module.

A detached editable delivery must keep `Project` and `Shared` beside each other: `Project/FlowFest.uproject`, `Shared/FlowArtsRuntime/FlowArtsRuntime.uplugin`, and `Shared/Tools`. Copy the generated project content and both game and plugin binaries as well as source; copying the `.uproject` alone is insufficient. Full source asset regeneration still requires the repository and its dependencies, as described above.

The current family schema is `flow-fest-prop-motion-v3`, captured with revision `clock-isolated-body-v3`. Character, physical prop family, and routine A/B must match exactly. Existing schema tags and `/Game/FlowFest` asset paths remain compatible during adoption. Moving assets to another mount requires an Unreal asset migration; renaming raw `.uasset` files is not supported. The prop converter's tested native Feet space is `(source.z, -source.x, source.y) * 100`; skeleton import and static prop mesh spaces have their own basis transformations and must not be treated as interchangeable.

### Sources

- `Tools/export-site.ts` reads the source terrain, runtime contract, species manifest, production dressing, grass, and ground-life ecology. It writes `Content/Data/site.json` and height samples in the source site's coordinate frame, preserving road and camp clearings.
- `Tools/prepare-assets.mjs` decodes source ecology and car GLBs and their textures. `--ground-only` restages just the ground detail textures, the ground-family mask and the farm-field map that `Tools/classify-fields.mjs` writes to `Content/Data/ground-fields.rg`. `Tools/author-assets.py` converts them to FBX and builds campsite and set meshes. `Tools/import-assets.py` imports those assets, preserves foliage transparency and double-sided surfaces, and creates native materials and the festival map.
- The parked lot uses the browser simulator's six car bodies, each sized to its measured length, settled on its four tyres, and given one of its catalogue paints per car; the driven car is the Ace '11 hatchback. Five bodies are by Daniel Zhabotinsky and the campervan by TheoClarke, all licensed [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/); titles and links are in `CREDITS.md` and the source repository's `static/models/flow-fest/cars/CREDITS.md`. `import-assets.py --only=Car/` reimports just the cars. `--only=Floor/` reimports the forest floor pieces (leaf litter, fern clump, fallen log and stump, authored in `author-assets.py`), `--only=Ground` rebuilds the ground material and `--only=Roads` the road materials.
- Camps match the browser simulator's mix of ridge tents, dome tents and pop-up canopies. Each tent's fly and each canopy top takes one of the web camp's fabric colours. The camp canopies carry a table, cooler and two chairs. The old gatehouse, workshop canopy, food canopy and their text signs are no longer placed; the real structures below replace them, and the `Gatehouse`, `Canopy` and `Signpost` set meshes stay in the content store unused. `author-assets.py -- --sets-only` rebuilds the set meshes without the source GLBs, and `import-assets.py --only=Set/` replaces them in the content store.
- Trees come from free Quixel Megaplants packs (`Tools/tree-sources.json`), added to the project from the editor's Fab window into `Content/Megaplant_Library/`. `Tools/grow-trees.py adopt` (run through `UnrealEditor-Cmd.exe <project> -run=pythonscript -script="<path> adopt"`) copies one shipped tree per family into `/Game/FlowFest/Assets/Trees/<family>/`: the pack's Nanite Foliage skeletal mesh with Dynamic Wind as `<family>_Live`, and a Nanite static mesh carrying the same leaf assembly (`UFlowFestTreeTools::CopyNaniteAssembly`) as `<family>_Far`. Species without a free pack borrow a tree of the right size (hornbeam for beech and hickory, black alder for walnut and sycamore, cypress for redcedar, dead pine for snags); the `FORMS` table in `grow-trees.py` says which. `grow-trees.py packs` lists the shipped trees with their heights, `measure` prints every family's height and crown ratio, `purge-eztree` deletes the old ez-tree assets. To grow a custom tree instead, `grow-trees.py graphs` copies each pack's Procedural Vegetation graph to `/Game/FlowFest/Vegetation/PV_<form>`; open it in the editor, run `py "<path>" configure <form> live` (then `far`) in the Cmd console, and press the toolbar Export button, because the exporter has no script entry. `bootstrap.ps1 -SkipTrees` skips the pack check. `[FlowFest.Trees] NearLayer=Far` in `DefaultGame.ini` switches the near layer to the static meshes. The existing Flow Fest source scene provides the tree instances.
- The festival's real structures come from Austen's site interview (`docs/superpowers/specs/flow-fest-sim/site-labels-interview.md`) and his marker file. `Tools/author-structures.py` builds them in Blender and `Tools/import-structures.py` imports them with their `M_Structures_` materials into `/Game/FlowFest/Assets/Structures`. `FlowFestStructures.h` holds the site plan: in Middle Earth the teal-roofed showcase stage facing the fire circle, the 10 m juggling tent, the white food pavilion on its concrete pad with two food booths and four picnic tables, volunteer HQ and first-aid pop-ups, the dip station, the entrance archway, the walk-in board and trampled strip, workshops W1 to W4 with six chairs and a lettered board each, and toilets with a shower trailer on gravel; on the lower loop the main building, the check-in canopy on the driver's left with its table, cash box, wristbands, clipboard, chairs, cooler, CHECK IN board and cones, a one-way counter-clockwise sign at the junction, three toilet blocks (one with showers) and W5; the gambrel barn, the abandoned stage, eight vendor booths along the walk and in the woods clearing south of it; and up top the pink cabin, the grey building, the log main cabin, a lodge with its porch, W7 and the west parking pad. The walk from the lower loop to Middle Earth carries rope spans every 6 to 10 m, tied to trees beside it or to stakes, hung with painted panels, hoops with streamers, or unlit paper lanterns. Trees standing inside a roof are the canopy scan reading the roof and are cleared. `FlowFest.Structures.*` tests keep every footprint off the roads, foot paths, gate trigger and spawn, and each other.
- `Tools/prepare-characters.mjs`, `convert-characters.py`, and `import-characters.py` port the deployed avatar catalog and source locomotion. The capture harness ports production flow and mounted poses into native animation assets. Walking, running, and directional movement match playback cadence to travel speed; jump and landing playback start at the source contact events. Terrain foot correction and contact-triggered footsteps run while walking.
- `Tools/prepare-props.mjs`, `convert-props.py`, and `import-props.py` port the physical prop catalog, including authored GLBs and source procedural shapes. `prepare-prop-motion.mjs` compacts corrected family captures into version 3, 28-number frames: two prop-root transforms, two wrist anchors, and two visual-body quaternions. Compact files retain the raw capture SHA-256.
- `Tools/prepare-audio.py` synthesizes six original mono ambient loops; `Tools/import-audio.py` imports them as looping Unreal sound waves.
- `Tools/convert-euc.py` reproduces the source electric-unicycle geometry and materials. `import-euc.py` stores the body and rotating wheel separately.
